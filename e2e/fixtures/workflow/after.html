10 · Architect

Tickets

The work is cut into thin tickets, each one built and merged on its own.

Tickets with blocking edges. The breakdown, by confirmation: you confirm it in the session that made it, with any change asked for there, then the tickets publish with no separate gate, and nothing is left waiting on an approval in another session or on another day.

Then, for each ticket: steps 11 to 13

11 · Developer

12 · Reviewer

Review chain

Once CI passes, a fresh session checks the PR against its ticket.

13 · You

Merge

You merge the PR once its ticket is met and the review approves.

Rules

Gates

  • A ticket gets at most two rounds: each verdict on the PR that sent it back is one.
  • Approval gates are yours: a step is approved when you approve it, not when its output exists.
  • A document is approved when it ends with the line Approved by <name>, which the step's skill writes when you approve it in the session; step 13 is approved by the merge.
  • A document without that line is waiting for approval, however finished it looks.
  • Steps 4 to 8 and 13 have one, step 8 only when it runs: later steps build on what they produce.
  • Step 10 has a confirmation instead: /to-tickets shows the breakdown in the session that made it, you confirm it there, and the tickets publish with nothing waiting on an approval.
  • A later step that finds an approved document wrong proposes the change.
  • Step 8 runs when the project map gives interface designs a home.
